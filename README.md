# Enhancing-Clinical-Decision-Making-with-explainable-AI-For-Diabetic-Retinopathy

 Introduction
 
 Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) corresponds to an exceedingly common and extremely
 dangerous complication of diabetes involving retinal damages due to high blood
 sugar levels. If undiagnosed and untreated, it could further translate into severe
 visual impairment and blindness [2]. DR happens to be a major vision-impairing
 disorder of public health concern today among working-age adults. It was chronic
 hyperglycemia and hypertension that would have caused the damage to small fine
 blood vessels inside the retina[3]. This would generally lie behind the eye on a very
 densed blood vessel network for oxygen and nutrient supply in order for it to func
tion. Once deprived by diabetic insult, those regions of the retina act functionally
 impaired and hence give you vision problems. Diabetic Retinopathy progresses very
 slowly almost always, does not show well-manifested symptoms at earlier stages,
 and therefore needs urgent diagnosis and timely treatment for effective management.
 The latest data from 2010 by the National Eye Institute in the US shows that about
 5.4 of the population are afflicted with DR, showing how common the condition can
 be amongthegeneral population[4]. This statistic must alert us to increase awareness
 of the disease and increase availability and accessibility of such tools for diagnosis
 and treatment. Lifestyle modification should especially be encouraged if DR is de
tected early and treatments are begun. Key lifestyle modifications include weight
 management, moderation of alcohol intake, smoking cessation, and exercise. The
 modifications slow DR progression and help with the general well-being of diabetic
 patients. More aggressive interventions such as laser therapy, intravitreal injections,
 and surgery of the eye are necessary for those patients affected by advanced DR. The
 aim is to avoid further vision loss and to stabilize the condition; however, they can
 carry some disadvantages and limitations.
